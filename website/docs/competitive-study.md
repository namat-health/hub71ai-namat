## Shared playbook

The following is design interpretation from the three studies, not a medical claim.

1. **Make prevention personal.** Open with a human ambition, then connect it to a concrete next step. Imagery creates warmth before the reader encounters technical detail.
2. **Make the unfamiliar legible.** A short sequence explains the recurring model. Testing is the beginning of an ongoing relationship rather than the entire story.
3. **Make the service tangible.** Established businesses use product screens, test catalogues, practical logistics and inclusion lists to show what membership buys.
4. **Layer evidence.** Clinical identities, process explanations, members, press and precise credentials answer different kinds of doubt. These depend on evidence the company actually possesses.
5. **Repeat a consistent action.** Sticky navigation and CTAs after meaningful explanations shorten the route back to signup. They do not require a different incentive in every section.
6. **Resolve practical friction near commitment.** Price, eligibility, locations, FAQs and what happens next sit near the conversion point.
7. **Give lower-intent visitors a lighter action.** Guides, email capture and international waitlists retain interest without demanding an immediate purchase.

This synthesis draws on the observed [Everlab homepage](https://www.everlab.com.au/), [Superpower homepage](https://superpower.com/) and [Function homepage](https://www.functionhealth.com/). See the individual studies for full module order and evidence.

## Where they differ

| Dimension | Everlab | Superpower | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sales shape | Small initial baseline, then a more tailored clinical plan. | A single membership developed into a broad continuing ecosystem. | Simple annual testing offer with explicit economic framing. |
| Price disclosure | Deferred from homepage into explanation and checkout. | Visible early and repeated in value inventory and buy box. | Early daily/annual framing, then a concentrated membership card. |
| Explanation style | Clinical service panels, app features, discovery choices. | Immersive numbered product chapters. | Direct process, breadth, tracking and practical logistics. |
| Trust emphasis | Named doctors and clinical depth. | Clinicians plus member, athlete, creator and press layers. | Medical/scientific board, member experience and operating scale. |
| Visual voice | Serif warmth within a restrained clinical system. | Warm cinematic imagery within a light sans-serif interface. | Editorial serif, ivory/terracotta and cinematic human scenes. |
| Motion | Video, ribbons, stacked panels and carousels. | Video, scrolling product chapters and story cycles. | Hero motion with pause control and alternating visual/information blocks. |

Visual observations: [Everlab](https://www.everlab.com.au/), [Superpower](https://superpower.com/), [Function](https://www.functionhealth.com/). Inspected fonts were Tobias/Saans, NB International Pro, and Financier Display/FT Base respectively. Namat uses separately licensed open typefaces, not competitor font assets.

## What this means for Namat

Borrow the clarity, rhythm and consistency. Scale the page to the evidence available. Namat needs to explain a planned approach and invite interest; it does not yet need to justify purchasing a finished membership.

The five designs therefore share a restrained set of sections: proposition, idea, audience, planned care cycle, the two founders, current stage and waitlist. The [adaptation plan and exact claim ledger](https://namat-website-git-design-five-directions-namat-health.vercel.app/research/adaptation/) show what was kept, changed or omitted.

## Research limits

This was a public desktop-site study, not a medical-evidence review, purchase, accessibility audit or conversion experiment. No signup was submitted. Geographical prompts, promotional overlays and live offers can change. The estimated purpose of each module is explicitly a design interpretation, not a claim about measured conversion performance.
