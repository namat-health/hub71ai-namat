# Everlab competitive study

Accessed 17 September 2026. Official pages only. Homepage order and visuals were checked in live Chrome; offer details were cross-checked against official pages and the public checkout. No registration, form submission, purchase, or account access occurred. These are observations of Everlab's marketing, not independently validated medical outcomes and not claims available to Namat.

The live homepage uses a Tobias serif hero, Saans sans-serif body, darkened clinical video, white/gray fields, coral accents, pill buttons, rounded panels, and generous spacing. ## Offer and audience detail

The explanation page introduces a $299 Health Baseline: historical records, health/risk analysis, a 30-minute doctor consultation, and app access. It presents a refund assurance linked to terms. The next stage is a doctor-informed, individually selected plan. Example patient profiles address new parenthood, metabolic/family-history concerns, and perimenopause/cancer-family-history concerns; this gives visitors several ways to recognise themselves.

Displayed packages include Diagnostics at $900 and Protocol at $2,700. Diagnostics lists 100+ biomarkers, DEXA, heart assessment, organ-age insights, doctor review, and a personalised plan; Protocol expands into physical/nutrition assessment and retesting. These are separate from the introductory step, not five interchangeable homepage membership tiers.

The page sequence is introductory promise → member quote → baseline → personalised examples → diagnostic menu → welcome/member video → testing/results/action/progress → repeated baseline CTA → FAQs. The FAQ addresses eligibility, referrals, timings, locations, and costs. Its marketing makes the initial commitment smaller than the eventual personalised programme. [How it works](https://www.everlab.com.au/how-it-works)

The public checkout currently shows $299 including GST, with a 40-marker panel across eight groups, record collection, a 30-minute doctor review, app access, member pricing, AI assistance, and biological-age tracking. A short completion-time headline and prominent refund reassurance sit beside the order. This is the most concrete observed entry offer. The inspected pages display **$**, not a currency code: Australian-dollar context is likely, but no currency conversion or equivalence should be assumed. [Public checkout](https://start.everlab.com.au/steps/checkout)

## Trust system

The doctor directory gives names, portraits, qualifications, clinical interests, and biographies. For example, Steven Lu is identified as co-founder/Chief Medical Officer with MBBS (Hons), DCH, FRACGP; Matthew Kent as imaging lead with MD, BMedHlthSc. It includes general practitioners, oncology, sports medicine, and other expertise. This supplies inspectable identity rather than an anonymous claim of medical expertise. Institution-logo presentation is credential context, not evidence of a Namat partnership. [Doctor directory](https://www.everlab.com.au/doctors)

Everlab's press centre includes financing announcements, international expansion, a Kearney partnership item, an AFL Players' Association announcement, and a Scott Pendlebury documentary. These are brand-issued items; their existence should not be mistaken for independent validation of medical effectiveness. The footer also gives an explicit screening-limitation statement. Together, press and boundary-setting make the business feel established. [Press centre](https://www.everlab.com.au/press)

A separate employer-programme page repeats member-result metrics, scale figures, named member stories, and specific app/privacy explanations. The design mechanism is layered reassurance: reported outcomes, operational scale, recognisable people, clinical interpretation, and clear privacy boundaries. All figures are **Everlab-reported** and unsuitable for reuse as Namat evidence. [Employer programme](https://get-started.everlab.com.au/)

## International waitlist funnel

The location prompt links to a separate international page. Its desktop layout places a serif promise and short description above an email/country/phone form on the left, with clinical footage on the right. It shows a global community-size claim, company contact, and social links. The primary button is “Join Waitlist”; no price or launch date appears in the inspected screen. In this session, the country default on the destination was United Kingdom despite the prior UAE prompt—an observable handoff inconsistency, not proof of intended targeting. No fields were filled. ## Transferable design interpretation for Namat

- **Keep the hierarchy:** a comprehensible promise, an immediate waitlist action, a short explanation, substantiated founder context, another opportunity to sign up.
- **Adapt the persuasion:** explain the venture's supported intention and approach; do not visually imply an operational clinic, available app, established care team, or delivered outcomes.
- **Use varied visual roles:** a large atmospheric composition can create feeling; a simple process diagram can clarify the idea; founder identity can provide accountability when supported by the deck.
- **Reduce friction:** one waitlist action and minimal requested information fit pre-launch evaluation. A mandatory phone field is a design choice, not a required convention.
- **Drop unsupported machinery:** pricing ladders, test counts, member statistics, testimonials, institutional badges, referral funnels, treatment menus, diagnostic screenshots, and scarcity. No competitor number, quote, clinician, benefit guarantee, or launch promise belongs in Namat's copy.
- **Preserve honesty through layout:** a sparse, intentional page is preferable to filling an established-company template with invented proof. Avoid decorative UI that visitors could mistake for existing product functionality.

## Research cautions

Everlab has multiple simultaneously indexed acquisition pages with different packages and inclusions. This study prioritises the current homepage, its explanation page, and the visible public checkout rather than treating a search snippet as one universal offer. Refund language on marketing pages and generic terms is not fully consistent; this report records the persuasion mechanism without representing a contractual guarantee.
